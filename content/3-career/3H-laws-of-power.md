# Laws of Power

Distilled rules for navigating power, social dynamics, and strategic positioning. Part career, part philosophy.

---

## General

1. When possible, extend olive branches to previous opponents — they often over-perform.
2. Keep secrets from everyone. Especially those closest to you.
3. Your clarity is their weapon. If they see your endgame, they can block it. Move like a ghost — clear to you, unclear to them.
4. Cultivate mystery. Pause before answering. Edit your speech like a poet, not a journalist.
5. You are a brand. Everything you do is a brand extension. The market will assign you a value whether or not you curate it.
6. Become impossible to ignore. Dress distinctively. Speak with timing.
7. Offer selective access, not availability.
8. Never appear eager — let others reinitiate.
9. When tempted to argue, execute instead. Build over debate. Show receipts.
10. If attacked, don't respond — overachieve.
11. Deliver visible wins quickly, especially after being challenged.
12. Distance swiftly from chaos — even if it's family.
13. Power comes from being irreplaceable. Create value that cannot be replicated — emotional, intellectual, strategic — and guard the formula.
14. Become the connector. Introduce people to opportunity, make them feel seen, but never give them the full playbook. Stay necessary, not convenient.
15. One act of calculated sincerity buys limitless trust. Honesty is a weapon when used sparingly.
16. Own a small mistake before it's noticed. It earns forgiveness.
17. In group settings, appear fascinated by others. Most people crave attention — they'll reveal what you can later use.
18. If someone ghosts, humiliates, or betrays you: don't go back for closure. Upgrade your life visibly. Never re-engage.

## In Professional Contexts

- Don't be the most available person — be the most desired.
- Frame your accomplishments as the natural result of talent, not a desperate struggle.
- Work on visible projects with quantitative outcomes on important metrics.
- Never do extra work without making it known.
- Act like a peer, never a subordinate.
- After a final interview: send a thank-you note, then go silent. Your absence creates scarcity.

## On Reputation

- Reputation is not your resume. It's the professional "scent" you leave behind.
- One great micro-project sent to a hiring manager > 20 portal applications.
- Don't scatter-gun your applications. Concentrate your forces.
