# Interview Prep System

**Philosophy:** Don't prep on the day — prep in layers. Goal is to feel over-prepared on content so you can be fully present in the room.

## Timeline

### 7+ Days Before (Deep Prep)
- Research the company: mission, recent news, portfolio (if VC/impact), key people
- Understand the role: what problem does this hire solve?
- Map your experience to key requirements — write 3–5 STAR stories
- Prepare answers to the 10 core behavioural questions below
- Research the interviewer on LinkedIn if you know who it is

### 3–5 Days Before
- Practice answers out loud or record yourself
- Prepare 5–10 smart questions to ask them
- Review domain knowledge gaps (finance basics, ESG metrics, impact frameworks, VC terminology)
- Run a mock interview or record on video

### 1–2 Days Before
- Light review only — don't cram
- Re-read your CV; be ready to speak to every line
- Prepare outfit, logistics, route/Zoom link
- Write your 3 "I want them to remember this" points
- Get good sleep

### Day Of
- No new prep — trust the work
- Morning routine as normal
- Briefly review your 3 key points
- Arrive/log on 5–10 min early

## Domain Learning Timeline

| Domain | Days Needed | How |
|---|---|---|
| VC fundamentals | 3–5 | Venture Deals, a16z / Sequoia blog posts, pitch deck teardowns |
| Impact investing | 3–5 | IFC/GIIN frameworks, ESG metrics, blended finance structures |
| Financial modelling basics | 5–7 | CFI free courses, model a company's P&L and DCF |
| ESG/sustainability frameworks | 2–3 | GRI, SASB, TCFD summaries — know acronyms and what they measure |
| Sector-specific (energy, healthcare) | 3–5 | Top 5 reports from that sector, key players |

## Your 2-Minute Story
Cover: where you started and what shaped you → what you've built / done (evidence) → why this role / why now → what you bring that others don't. Polish before any first interview. Practice until it sounds natural, not rehearsed.

## Salary Negotiation
- Research market rates before every process (Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary)
- Know your floor, target, and aspirational number
- Don't give a number first: *"I'd love to understand the full package before discussing — what's the range budgeted for this role?"*

## Core Behavioural Questions (STAR — 90 sec to 2 min max)

1. **Tell me about yourself.** 2-min structured: background → what you've built → why this role → what you bring
2. **Why do you want to work here?** Specific, researched — tie their work to your values or experience
3. **Time you led without formal authority.** Shows: leadership, influence, initiative
4. **Time you failed. What did you learn?** Shows: self-awareness, resilience, growth mindset. Real but not catastrophic.
5. **Decision with incomplete information.** Shows: judgment, comfort with ambiguity — crucial for VC/impact roles
6. **Difficult stakeholder or disagreement.** Shows: emotional intelligence, professionalism
7. **Most complex problem you've worked on.** Shows: analytical thinking, structured problem-solving
8. **Worked under pressure or tight deadlines.** Shows: resilience, prioritisation, execution
9. **Time you learned something quickly.** Shows: learning agility — important for sector pivots
10. **Where do you want to be in 5 years?** Shows: ambition, self-awareness, alignment. Honest but strategic.

---
> **The mindset:** Interviews are designed to disqualify you, not qualify you. Your job is to find every reason they'd say no — and either fix it or have a plan for it.

## 📚 Reference Bank

_Read this before filling in anything below. These are examples and prompts — not scripts. Take what's true, rewrite everything in your own voice._

- Common disqualifying factors — and how to address them
    
    **Gap: No direct sector experience (e.g. housing, defense, fintech, deep tech)**
    
    Plan: Do a 3-hour crash course. Read 2 industry reports, learn 10 terms of art, understand 1 key deal structure or regulation. Be ready to say: "I came in knowing X but not Y — here's what I've done in the last 48 hours and how I learn fast."
    
    **Gap: I haven't done this exact role before**
    
    Plan: Map your closest experience to the role's actual day-to-day. Be specific. "I haven't done X formally, but at [place] I did Y which required the same underlying skill of Z."
    
    **Gap: Too generalist / no deep vertical**
    
    Plan: Own it as a feature. "I'm deliberately cross-sector because I think the most interesting opportunities sit at intersections. Here's how that's shown up in my work."
    
    **Gap: Academic / not enough operator experience**
    
    Plan: Lead with CleanCrop and New Leaf — these are operator-adjacent. Don't let them define you as a student. Anchor on what you built, shipped, or closed.
    
    **Gap: Based in India / timezone mismatch**
    
    Plan: Address it early if it's remote. Have a concrete answer about overlap hours, async work style, and why it's not a problem.
    
    **Gap: Early career, limited track record**
    
    Plan: Compensate with specificity. Vague = junior. The more precisely you describe what you did, how you thought, and what happened — the more credible you are.
    
    **Gap: No network in this space**
    
    Plan: Show you're building one deliberately. Name people you've spoken with, what you're learning from them.
    
- Common sectors to learn fast — key terms, sources, time needed
    
    **Housing / real estate**
    
    Terms: cap rate, NOI, LTV, AMI, LIHTC, HUD, zoning, inclusionary zoning, rent stabilization, YIMBY
    
    How it works: developers + capital stack + regulatory environment + tenants. Learn where money flows and where it gets stuck.
    
    Sources: Planetizen, Urban Institute, NMHC, a16z housing policy pieces
    
    Time: 3–4 hours to be conversational
    
    **VC (generalist)**
    
    Terms: pre-seed/seed/Series A-C, SAFE, convertible note, cap table, pro-rata, LP/GP, carry, DPI, TVPI, IRR, vintage year
    
    How it works: GPs raise from LPs → deploy into startups → return via exits (IPO or M&A)
    
    Sources: Strictly VC, Sacra, Meritech benchmarks, Crunchbase, firm blogs
    
    Time: 1–2 hours if you know basics
    
    **Climate / energy transition**
    
    Terms: PPA, IRA tax credits, additionality, carbon offsets, avoided emissions, baseload, curtailment, REC, blended finance, concessional capital
    
    Sources: RMI, BloombergNEF, Heatmap News, CTVC newsletter
    
    Time: 1–2 hours (you know the basics — refresh on IRA deal structures)
    
    **Agri / food tech**
    
    Terms: yield, input costs, offtake agreement, supply chain traceability, regenerative ag, precision fermentation, alternative protein
    
    How it works: farm → supply chain → processor → retailer → consumer. Startups attack inputs, processing, or distribution.
    
    Sources: AgFunder, Good Food Institute, FAIRR
    
    Time: 2–3 hours
    
    **Defense / dual-use tech**
    
    Terms: SBIR, OTA (Other Transaction Authority), ITAR, prime contractor, dual-use, autonomy, ISR, C2, program of record
    
    How it works: DoD contracts → primes → subcontractors. New wave = software/AI going direct via OTA.
    
    Sources: Defense One, Breaking Defense, War on the Rocks, Anduril/Palantir investor materials
    
    Time: 4–5 hours if completely new
    
    **Fintech**
    
    Terms: interchange, BIN sponsorship, core banking, KYC/AML, neobank, embedded finance, ACH/RTP rails, BNPL, credit underwriting
    
    How it works: rails → software → distribution. Regulation is everything.
    
    Sources: Fintech Business Weekly, a16z fintech, Mary Meeker reports
    
    Time: 3–4 hours
    
- Why do I want this job — prompts to find the real answer
    
    _The interviewer knows when you're performing. These questions help you find what's actually true._
    
    - What would I be doing in this role that I'm not doing now?
    - Is there something specific about how this company operates that I want to learn?
    - Do I actually believe in what they're building? Why?
    - Is the draw the company, the role, the team, or the learning? (All are valid — but know which it is.)
    - Would I be disappointed if I didn't get it?
    
    Answers that land in interviews:
    
    - "This is exactly the intersection of X and Y that I've been trying to find — most roles are one or the other."
    - "I've been following [specific thesis/person] for [reason] and this feels like the right moment to be inside that rather than adjacent to it."
    - "I want to build the muscle of [X skill] in a serious environment, and this is one of the few places I'd actually get to do that."
- Why this company specifically — how to not sound generic
    
    "I admire your mission" is the death of a good answer. Instead:
    
    - Reference something specific: a portfolio company, a deal, an essay, a hire, a thesis shift
    - Connect it to your own work: "When I was at [X], I kept running into [problem] — your approach to [Y] is the most interesting answer I've seen."
    - Show you did the work: "I spoke with [person] about their experience here, and what they said about [X] confirmed what I'd already thought."
    - Acknowledge the moment: "Given where [sector] is right now, this feels like exactly the right time to be here rather than [adjacent thing]."
- Why am I a good fit — how to say it without being generic
    
    Avoid: "I'm a hard worker, fast learner, and passionate about impact." Pick 1–2 specific things and be concrete:
    
    - "I've done [specific thing] which maps directly to [what this role needs]."
    - "Most candidates won't have [X]. I do, and here's why that matters for what you're building."
    - "I've been in [specific situation] before — I know how to navigate [challenge this company faces]."
    
    Your genuine edges:
    
    - You've been on both capital and operator sides (New Leaf + CleanCrop)
    - You have South Asia exposure, which is underrepresented in most VC rooms
    - You can hold quantitative rigor and relationship-building at the same time
    - You're constitutionally systems-oriented — you build infrastructure, not just execute tasks
- Nervous questions — the most common ones and how to think about them
    
    **"Why do you have so many different experiences? What's the thread?"**
    
    Don't apologize for breadth. Name the thread: you've been building a specific point of view through different vantage points.
    
    **"Tell me about a failure."**
    
    Pick something real. The failure isn't the point — your analysis of what went wrong and what you'd do differently is. Show you can look at yourself clearly.
    
    **"Where do you want to be in 5 years?"**
    
    You don't need a perfect answer. You need a directional one: "I want to be [X] and this role accelerates that because [Y]."
    
    **"Do you have other offers / processes?"**
    
    Honest without oversharing: "I have a few conversations going, but I'm being selective — this is one I'm particularly serious about because [specific reason]."
    
    **"Why are you leaving your current situation?"**
    
    For you: graduating is clean. "I'm at a transition point and being deliberate about where I go next — this is at the top of that list because [reason]."
    
    **"What do you know about [domain]?"**
    
    Don't fake it. "My direct experience is [X]. I've been learning [Y] — here's what I understand so far. What would you add?" Intellectual honesty + curiosity beats performance.
    
    **"Tell me about a time you had to learn something completely new quickly."**
    
    You have strong answers: CleanCrop in an agri context you weren't from, carbon markets at New Leaf. Use one.
