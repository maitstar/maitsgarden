# Career Rules

Rules for navigating hiring, power dynamics, and professional positioning. Distinct from principles (which are about identity and craft) — these are tactical and strategic.

---

## Identity & Positioning

- You are either an analyst or an operator. Figure out which one you are. The analyst sees patterns, synthesises, builds frameworks. The operator executes and moves. Most people try to be both and become neither.
- You need to be elite at one skill and top 25% at another to be a prize. In careers: investor, operator, or manager. Know your lane.
- Having a brand name on your resume opens up roles. Get the signal, then leverage it.
- Your reputation is not your resume. It's the professional "scent" you leave behind — built by high-quality questions, concise thank-you notes, and courtesy extended to schedulers and assistants.
- Don't scatter-gun your applications. Identify 5–10 dream companies and concentrate your forces. Customize every interaction for those specific targets.
- Don't declare on LinkedIn that you are "#OpenToWork." Conduct a stealth search. Your true intention isn't "to find a job," it's "to solve a specific problem at a specific company."

## The Job Search as Strategy

- Jobs come from being *known*, not from applying.
- One great micro-project sent to a hiring manager > 20 portal applications.
- Frame your abilities as tools that will make your future manager look like a visionary to their own superiors.
- When asking for a referral: provide a pre-written editable email they can send. Make it easy.
- Referrals: ask for *advice*, not *jobs*. Be specific. Act on what they say.
- Research your interviewers on LinkedIn for their *tone and posts*, not just their work history. Understand if they are a formal traditionalist or a casual disruptor.
- Play the "naive student" during informational interviews. People reveal far more when they're teaching.
- Use informational interviews to gather intelligence: internal challenges, team dynamics, unstated priorities.

## Interviews

- Signpost in interviews — say things that make them want to ask the next question.
- Interviews are designed to disqualify. Your job is to pass every filter, then stand out.
- Don't just rely on STAR. Introduce a counter-intuitive insight or challenge a common assumption in their industry. This marks you as an innovator.
- Listen for the interviewer's "thumbscrew" — the thing they complain about most. Frame your skills as the solution.
- For final-round: create a "30-60-90 Day Plan" and present it. You are selling them a vision of the future with you in it.
- Present complex work as a simple, elegant story of success. Accomplishments should seem like the natural result of talent, not a desperate struggle.
- Connect your skills to the company's mission statement. You're not just an analyst; you're helping them achieve something significant.
- In a group interview, identify the core concern of the hiring manager and address it directly.
- When a recruiter asks an illegal question (age, family), mirror it back: *"How does that relate to the responsibilities of the role?"*

## Negotiations & Offers

- In salary negotiations, state your number (well-researched, confident) and stop talking. Let silence hang.
- When asked about salary expectations, give a well-researched range to anchor the negotiation.
- Politely decline "exploding offers" that give you 24 hours to decide. A company that respects you gives you time.
- When you get the offer you want, accept it and stop looking.
- In victory, know when to stop. Squeezing out every last drop can sour your entry.

## Power & Reputation

- Keep secrets from everyone. Especially those closest to you.
- Cultivate mystery. Pause before answering. Edit your speech like a poet, not a journalist.
- Never appear eager — let others reinitiate.
- When tempted to argue, execute instead. Build over debate. Show receipts.
- If attacked, don't respond — overachieve.
- Deliver visible wins quickly, especially after being challenged.
- Distance swiftly from chaos — even if it's family.
- One act of calculated sincerity buys limitless trust.
- Own a small mistake before it's noticed. It earns forgiveness.
- If someone ghosts or betrays you: upgrade your life visibly. Never re-engage.

## After the Decision

- After a rejection: gracious thank-you note → reopens the door.
- After a final interview: send a thank-you note, then go silent. Your absence while other candidates pester them creates scarcity.
- If rejected for a role you truly wanted: don't show disappointment. Focus visibly on the next thing.
