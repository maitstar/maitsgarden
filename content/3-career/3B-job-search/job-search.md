# Job Search System

## Strategic Frame

**Job interviews are designed to disqualify.** Your job: survive every filter, then stand out.

**Jobs come from being known, not from applying.** The portal is the last resort, not the strategy.

**Broad funnel, tight filter.** Explore many sectors/firms. Apply only to roles you'd be genuinely excited to take.

**Tailor your persona completely for each role:**
- Startup: scrappy generalist
- Large corp: polished specialist
- VC: pattern-matcher with a thesis
- Impact org: values-aligned practitioner

---

## The Application Edge (Non-Obvious Moves)

1. **Micro-project method:** Create something that solves a small problem for the company — a one-page market analysis, a model, a mockup. Send it directly to the hiring manager. Bypasses the portal entirely.
2. **Post-rejection grace note:** Gracious thank-you expressing continued interest transforms you from rejected candidate to potential future hire.
3. **Weakness framing:** Offer a genuine but non-critical flaw you've actively worked to improve. Self-aware and human.
4. **Referral ask:** Don't ask for a job. Ask for *advice*. Be specific. Act on it. Report back. That's how an informational becomes a champion.

## Job Application Prompt (for AI)

```
I'm applying for the following job.

[Paste full job description]

Help me with strategic, non-obvious tips to gain an edge:

1. What is this company/role actually optimising for beneath the job description language?
2. What are the 3 most important things this hiring team wants to see?
3. What would make my application stand out from 200 other applicants?
4. What's the most dangerous assumption I could make about this role?
5. What industry context should I understand before my interview?
```

---

## Referral Process

1. Ask for advice (specific question, not "any jobs?")
2. Act on their advice
3. Report back: *"I took your advice on X — here's how it's going. Could I update you over a call?"*
4. Repeat, building on each person

---

## Sunk Cost Traps — Cut These

- Networking coffee from 3 weeks ago with a mid-tier contact
- Informational interview with a company you're no longer excited about
- Applications to roles you'd only take out of desperation
- "I should apply because it's climate-adjacent" without genuine interest
