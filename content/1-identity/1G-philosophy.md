## Personal Operating Principles

**Privacy.** Stay private about: plans for the future, how far along you are in anything, business or creative ideas. Don't share to get validation — do first, share results. The dopamine hit of announcement is borrowed from the work.

**Find your Rome.** You cannot enter a situation you don't know and act however you want. Find where you fit, embed yourself there, work within the structure that exists. Don't start big.

**The lie to yourself principle.** Simply noticing the lie begins to dissolve it. The most common ones: "I'll start tomorrow." "I don't really need help." "It doesn't matter." "I'm fine."

**Free passes.** Everyone gets 1–2 free passes for genuine mistakes — not for disrespect, dishonesty, patterns, or major violations. Track it: first time = "Interesting, I'll watch for patterns." Second time = "This might be who they are." Third time = "This IS who they are."

**You are shaped by what you absorb.** Audit inputs ruthlessly: what you read, watch, follow, listen to, the conversations you have. Curate for inspiration, not comparison.

**Only reach out when you have something to say.** Quality over frequency. Don't broadcast constantly. Reach out with substance — an article, a thought, genuine curiosity, real news. The connection becomes valuable because it's real, not because it's regular.

**Fragility = openness to experience.** Reframe vulnerability as a feature. The things that make you feel open and exposed — capacity for feeling, willingness to try new things, emotional responsiveness — are the same things that create depth, learning, and connection. The work is managing overwhelm, not closing down.

**System-building compounds.** Infrastructure built once returns value forever without daily willpower. Automations, protocols, decision trees, habits — they're not shortcuts. They're leverage. Build the system, then the system works for you.

**Q2 question: What's the one thing you're executing, not just optimising?** Don't get lost in iteration and refinement. Identify the core work and execute it. Optimization comes after you have something real.

## Theory of Self: How Identity Actually Works

**Identity is built through repeated action, not discovered through introspection.** You don't find yourself — you build yourself. You write, and then become a writer. Action creates identity.

**Every day you don't choose is a choice to stay the same.** Waiting is choosing. There is no right time. Ready is a myth.

**Engineer the default.** Willpower is finite. Design your environment so you don't need it. Make good choices automatic, make bad choices require effort.

**Stack momentum before going hard.** Start small, stack changes, build the habit before increasing intensity. Let success build on success.

**Have theme days.** Each cognitive context switch costs 20–30 minutes of capacity. Dedicate specific days to specific types of work. Batching keeps you in one cognitive mode all day.

**Jet Lag Theory — Your life is a compound result of the past 90 days.** Set 90-day goals, not yearly goals. Review, adjust, repeat.

## Career Philosophy: The Three Stages

**Stage 1 — Job (survival).** Working to pay bills, building basic skills. Goal: financial stability, skill development, learning what you don't want.

**Stage 2 — Career (development).** Intentional skill building, strategic moves, building reputation and network. Goal: become excellent at something valuable, build career capital.

**Stage 3 — Calling (work as play).** Work that energises you, operating at highest level, deep sense of meaning. Most people never reach Stage 3 because they skip the groundwork or expect passion to come first (it comes from mastery).

## Building Integrity

Integrity isn't about perfection — it's about the ongoing commitment to realignment.

**Start with radical self-honesty.** Integrity begins with telling yourself the truth before anyone else. Audit how you spent your time, energy, and money last week — does it reflect who you claim to be?

**Define your non-negotiables specifically enough to guide real decisions.** Vague principles like "be honest" are less useful than something like "I don't say yes when I mean no" or "I follow through on commitments within the timeframe I promise."

**Close the say-do gap.** Integrity erodes in small moments — the text you said you'd send, the deadline you let slip without acknowledging it. Treat your word as sacred, even in tiny things. This builds internal trust with yourself that compounds over time.

**Integrate across domains rather than compartmentalizing.** Are you the same person with a potential employer as you are with a close friend? With a stranger as with family? The places where you shape-shift are where integrity work is most needed.

**Use discomfort as a compass.** The moments where integrity is hardest — telling a difficult truth, admitting a mistake, holding a boundary when it's socially costly — are precisely the moments that build it. Svadharma (from the Gita): act in alignment with your nature regardless of outcome.

**Practice the pause.** Before reacting, committing, or responding — especially under pressure — create a small gap. Ask: what would the person I'm becoming do here?

**Forgive yourself for past gaps.** The Saturn return energy is ideal for this kind of deep recalibration. You're already doing the work by asking this question.

## Schwartz Theory of Basic Values

Values are complex. Understanding them gives you a framework for recognizing conflicts.

**Values are beliefs linked to affect.** When values are activated, they become infused with feeling.

**Values refer to desirable goals that motivate action.** They're not just abstract principles — they drive what you actually do.

**Values transcend specific actions and situations.** Unlike norms or attitudes that refer to specific actions/objects, values apply broadly across contexts.

**The theory identifies universal value dimensions that explain conflicts.** Two bipolar dimensions organize all human values:
- **Openness to change vs. Conservation:** Independence and novelty (self-direction, stimulation) versus order and stability (security, conformity, tradition)
- **Self-enhancement vs. Self-transcendence:** Pursuit of your own interests (power, achievement) versus concern for others' welfare (benevolence, universalism)

**Your self-identified values:** Self-direction, stimulation, security, then achievement and power. This means you value independence and novelty but with a safety net — you want freedom to explore but within controllable parameters.
