# Operating Manual

*Rules and observations for self, career, relationships, and systems. Distilled from practice.*

---

## 1. Self & Identity

- Close open loops. Shorten time to action.
- Act like the person you want to become.
- The way you treat yourself is the way people treat you.
- Radical self-responsibility is the foundation of all power.
- Energy goes where identity is.
- Anything a person chases in life runs away.
- Self-trust is built through keeping promises to yourself before keeping promises to others.
- Belonging is assumed until others prove you wrong.
- Radical self-love moonshots productivity — it improves the effort-to-outcome ratio.
- All anger is self-anger.
- All self-discovery happens in pursuit of something, because only then do you have a benchmark to measure yourself against.

---

## 2. Career & Productivity

- The magic you are looking for is in the work you are avoiding.
- Never put out work below the standard you want to be known for. Every output is marketing.
- Excellence requires being: low-maintenance, high-reliability, a proactive clarity engine, no ego, no drama.
- Confidence works backwards from competence. You can't think your way into it — only act your way into it.
- Being dependable beats being brilliant. Consistency creates compound opportunities.
- Attention and energy are finite. Winners are ruthless about protecting them.
- Mastery of anything requires being selfish with time.
- Speed is important in all endeavors.
- Decay happens faster than improvement. Good enough now often beats perfect later.
- Timing beats perfection because windows close faster than skills develop.
- Avoidance is a debt that compounds daily. Each day of delay makes the task emotionally heavier and practically harder.
- Perfectionism is procrastination in disguise. "I need to be fully prepared" means "I need to feel guaranteed of success," which never arrives.

---

## 3. Mental

- Important decisions should only be made when calm and clear. Anxiety literally disables the frontal lobe.
- Anxiety is intelligence without direction. Channel it toward specific action or it becomes paralysis.
- There's always a constructive takeaway, even if it's only to hedge against bad luck better.

---

## 4. Relationships

### Core Principles

- True charisma combines genuine care for others' enjoyment with complete emotional detachment from their approval. Neediness kills charisma.
- Think of flirting not as trying to impress or convince — but filtering for people who are already a good match.
- Be worth choosing, not grateful to be chosen. Match energy, never exceed it. Make yourself easy to choose but impossible to take for granted.
- Trust what you observe, communicate needs once clearly, then make decisions based on responses — not on trying to convince someone to want you more.
- The moment you optimize for being liked, you optimize away from being respected.
- People don't leave relationships — they leave versions of themselves they don't like in those relationships.
- Boundaries aren't walls — they're price tags.

### Chase & Connection

- When others pull away, leaning back creates mystery and re-centers power.
- Quality over quantity in all connections.
- Why vagueness is attractive: labels create expectations, expectations create pressure, pressure kills authenticity.
- You should approach with max self-esteem and a serious discount for societal judgment. A lot of longing is for the desire to get picked. Real connection only happens once you move past this.

### Reputation & Power

- Reputation is built in drops and lost in buckets. One inconsistency can undo years of consistency.
- Your reputation travels faster than you do and arrives before you get there. Consistency across contexts matters more than perfection in any single context.
- Power comes from being irreplaceable. Create value that cannot be replicated.
- Power accumulates at the intersection of information asymmetry and decision authority. Information without authority is gossip; authority without information is incompetence.
- People's capacity to help you is inversely related to how much you need their help. Strength attracts opportunity, weakness repels it.
- Status is awarded to those who don't seem to need it. Power accumulates around people who appear indifferent to its accumulation.
- Status operates on a delay mechanism. People treat you based on who they think you were six months ago. Reinvention requires geographic or social distance — you need to reset the reference points.
- In any conflict, whoever needs the resolution least controls the negotiation.

### Influence & Trust

- People make decisions emotionally then justify them rationally. Win the emotion first, then give them the logic to feel smart about what they already want to do.
- People don't just remember what you said — they remember the emotional state you put them in. Your words are forgotten, but the feeling you created becomes their permanent association with your name.
- The person who sets the timeline controls the outcome.
- People fight positions but negotiate interests. Find the shared interest buried under opposing positions.
- Charisma: all you have to do is prompt a human, predict their response ahead of time, have a target response in mind, and see what would prompt that target response. Forget pre-planned scripts.
- People will believe whatever allows them to be the hero of their own story.

## 5. Systems

- Work backwards from the outcome you want — define where you want to be and plan backwards.
- Constantly ask: "How would someone world-class handle this?" Then copy them ruthlessly.
- The market responds to the price you set for yourself. Most people negotiate themselves down before anyone else gets the chance.
- Luck accumulates around people who position themselves in its path — the visible and accessible.
- Sunk cost fallacy is not real — every choice you make changes you.
- Reversible decisions can be made with incomplete information; irreversible ones require near-certainty.
- Decision fatigue isn't about quantity of choices — it's about constantly re-deciding things that should be automated.
- Communication is translation, not transmission. The burden of understanding falls on the speaker. Clarity is kindness.
- Undocumented learning dies with the moment. Insights not captured get re-learned indefinitely.
- The efficient frontier of any domain is at its edges, where conventional wisdom stops applying.
- Failure is feedback with an emotional surcharge. The person who can extract the signal from the noise of their emotions learns fastest.
- Your biggest advantages tomorrow will come from skills you're embarrassingly bad at today.
- Stress and anxiety feed on idleness. When you take action, you starve them. The answer is found in the action.

---

## 6. Health & Routine

- Daily actions shape the body you have.
- Physical health (sleep, nutrition, movement) is the baseline for emotional resilience.

---

## 7. Daily Execution

- Work on visible projects with quantitative outcomes on important metrics.
- Never do extra work without making it known.
- Obsess over the details of your projects because no one else will.
- Wrap up each day as if it's the end of your life.
- Interpret everything bad that happens as: (1) skill issue, (2) bad luck. There's always a constructive takeaway.
- Excellence is a habit that becomes invisible to its practitioner but obvious to everyone else.
- Treat your colleagues like clients.

### Execution & Urgency

- Time and attention are more valuable than money because they don't regenerate.
- Every yes is a no to something else. Choose yeses that compound.
- Urgency is a competitive advantage because most people mistake motion for progress.
- Reply within 24 hours or explicitly set expectations. Silence creates anxiety and erodes trust.
- The 70% rule: act when 70% ready, get the remaining 30% from doing.
- Motivation follows action, not the reverse.
