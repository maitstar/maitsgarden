# Routines and Systems

## The Core Principle

Make essential the default position. With routine and habit, mental work shifts to the basal ganglia — freeing up prefrontal cortex for things that actually need it. Every recurring decision you eliminate is working memory returned.

---

## Daily Structure

### Non-Negotiables
1. Have routines — same structure, same time, every day
2. Be on time — on time means 5 minutes early
3. Strict wake-up and bedtime — same time daily, including weekends
4. Workout every day — even a short walk counts
5. Keep the space clean — environment is cognition
6. Drink enough water with electrolytes
7. Intuitive eating
8. Eat whole foods at home
9. No caffeine after 3pm
10. Cut sugar

### Morning Routine

**Actual Protocol (from Morning_Routine.md):**
1. Dream log — record immediately on waking, before moving
2. Morning pages — 3 pages longhand, stream of consciousness
3. Meds
4. Coffee with shroom booster
5. Toast or small breakfast
6. Wheatgrass + psyllium husk cocktail
7. 5 min tai chi
8. Brush teeth, ablution, shower
9. Gita bibliomancy — open Bhagavad Gita to random page, read
10. Deep work block
11. Walk
12. Second deep work block
13. Journal

**Principles:**
- Nothing gets you out of bed like an important task or something you love — design morning around this
- Bright light therapy (10–20 min, 10,000 lux) within 1 hour of waking
- No carbs alone for breakfast — protein first (20–30g)
- Start the day's major task before anything reactive

### Power Down Ritual (30 minutes, end of workday)
1. **Clear open loops (10 min)** — go through emails, messages, to-do lists. Respond to anything <2 minutes. Schedule everything else.
2. **Review goal stack (5 min)** — briefly look at weekly goals
3. **Line up tomorrow's dominoes (10 min)** — identify 1–3 most critical actions for tomorrow. Write them somewhere prominent.
4. **Organise workspace (3 min)** — tidy desk, remove friction
5. **Verbal shutdown (2 min)** — say out loud: **"Work is done."**

### Evening Routine
- Habit tracking
- Writing/journaling
- No social media
- Close the house: "Do the laundry, do the dishes, shower. You're closed for business."

## Weekly Systems

### Sunday Planning
1. Write 3 key commitments for the week — assign deadlines
2. Load calendar with all non-negotiables first — see available free time
3. Pre-schedule tasks, study, errands
4. What is the ONE thing that would make the week a success?

### Weekly Values Behaviours Checklist

**Reliability:**
- Write 3 key commitments with deadlines each Sunday
- Reply to all messages within 24 hours
- Day before anything important: work 90% ready
- One proactive update per week to someone waiting on you
- Don't: overcommit. Honest no beats flaky yes.

**Excellence:**
- Pick ONE deliverable to spend 30% more time on
- Before sending: self-edit, catch 3+ improvements
- Get feedback from someone you respect
- Don't: ship work you'd be embarrassed about.

**Preparedness:**
- List what you need for 3 key events each Sunday
- Pre-mortem for anything with stakes
- One written backup plan
- Add 20% to every time estimate
- Don't: over-prepare at the expense of likely scenarios.

## GTD: Inbox Processing

**Is it actionable?**
- No → Reference / trash / someday-maybe
- Yes → Is it ASAP? <2 min? Multi-step? Delegatable? Meeting item? Calendar item? → Next Actions list

**O.H.I.O.:** Only Handle It Once — respond immediately, file permanently, or discard.

**Pattern planning:** Plug recurring obligations into fixed weekly slots. Frees planning capacity for variable tasks.

## Task Selection Algorithm

From `Reminders.md` — how to decide what to work on next:

**Default rule:** Do the shortest task first.

**Two exceptions:**
1. **Stalled tasks get a priority bump.** If you created a task weeks ago, or have been postponing it for many days in a row, it must be done now.
2. **Content-dependence:** If you're already working on a particular project, prioritise tasks from that project over the global to-do list.

**Additional rule from ADHD research:** Always estimate time when making a to-do list. Never write a task without a time estimate next to it. This prevents the list from feeling endless and unmanageable.

## Productivity Mechanics

- **Pomodoro (25/5):** best for inbox clearing, repetitive tasks, anything with a clear done-state
- **10-minute rule:** force 10 minutes. Usually you'll continue.
- **2-minute rule:** if <2 min, do it immediately
- **Theme days:** Monday = applications. Tuesday = deep work. Wednesday = calls/networking. Thursday = prep. Friday = review. Switching cognitive contexts costs 20–30 min each.
- **Habit stacking:** tie new behaviours to existing cues. Coffee → check calendar.

## Weekly Reset Day

One day a week (usually Sunday) — the full structure:

1. Write 3 key commitments with deadlines
2. Load calendar with all non-negotiables first — see available free time
3. Pre-schedule tasks, study, errands into those free blocks
4. Identify the ONE thing that would make the week a success
5. Set theme and intent for the week
6. Define 3-5 outcomes you want by end of week
7. Identify 3 things that could derail you — pre-mortem them
8. Run the weekly scorecard from [[goals-and-vision]] review template
9. Review Life Wheel for any domain falling below 5 — address it

**Goals clarity ritual (from Capture_Notes):** Write 5 things you want to accomplish this week, 5 this month, 5 this year. Keep on a post-it or pinned note.
