# Stop Oversharing

## The Three Root Causes

**1. Can't Stand Silence.** People who grew up in emotionally unstable households where silence preceded pain cope by filling blank spaces with information. In adulthood, silence still reads as the precursor to pain — so you fill it with personal information, almost always unnecessary.

**2. Insecurity.** An insecure person tries to establish security with others by making themselves likeable. They divulge as much information as possible, hoping one piece will create common ground and therefore relationship security. This is the seeking-approval trope.

**3. The Misunderstood Pattern.** People raised with narcissistic parents who were "always right" over-explain as a defense mechanism. When you were constantly misinterpreted as a child, you grew into an adult who explains things that didn't need explaining. Every conflict is a trigger for the child whose voice was hushed in favor of judgment.

At the core of all three: **fear and the desire for security.**

---

## How to Fix It

**1. Exposure Therapy.** Phobia is trained in the presence of its trigger. Place yourself in situations that trigger the oversharing, and consciously choose not to. Practice: respond to "How was school?" with "Good, actually. Had a great time. How are you?" Full stop.

**2. CBT.** Cognitive Behavioral Therapy addresses behaviors by identifying and changing the thought patterns that drive them. Doable without a therapist, but better with one.

**3. Boundaries.** A boundary is the line of discontinuity where the rest of the world stops and you begin. You need these.

**4. Confidence.** At the heart of confidence is security. At the heart of oversharing is insecurity.

**5. Mindfulness / Mind Mastery.** When mentally focused on the now, you can catch yourself oversharing and stop. Consciousness is how you leave the childhood home that lives inside you.

**6. Privacy and Mystery.** Gatekeep information about yourself. Have they earned it? Learn to breadcrumb people.

**7. Listening.** Most oversharing comes from overtalking. Practice listening as a skill. Ask questions. Let others finish.

**8. A Full Life.** If you're oversharing partly because your life feels boring, that's information. Solo dates, classes, reading, cooking. When your life is actually interesting, you have things to actually share and less need to fill silences with random information.
