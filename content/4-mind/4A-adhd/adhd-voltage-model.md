## Concept

Mental energy is better modeled as **voltage** than as a battery. Different tasks have different threshold voltages required to begin or sustain them.

- **High-voltage tasks:** emotionally aversive tasks, creative open-ended work (high threshold to start)
- **Low-voltage tasks:** simple chores, routine actions

Voltage declines through the day as cognitive load accumulates.

## Key Principle

Unlike spoon theory (where energy is fungible across time), voltage determines which tasks are *accessible* at which time of day — not just how much energy remains.

## Practical Implications

- Schedule aversive and creative tasks early morning when voltage is highest
- Save chores, gym, low-threshold tasks for evening
- When voltage is below a task's threshold, no amount of willpower bridges the gap — switch tasks instead
- Task selection: do shortest tasks first to build momentum; give stalled tasks a priority bump

## Contrast with Spoon Theory

Spoon theory: fixed number of "spoons" (units of energy) to spend each day, fungible across tasks and time. Voltage model: the question isn't how much energy you have — it's whether you have enough voltage for *this specific task right now*.
